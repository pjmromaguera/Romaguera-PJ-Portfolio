Overview

Highlights

Where ARR eligibility and species-specific growing requirements have to agree.

Objective

  • Identify areas eligible under the ARR (Afforestation, Reforestation & Revegetation) methodology.
  • Determine which of those areas can be restored by planting coffee, cacao, or bamboo.
  • Support prioritization for nature-based restoration planning.

Approach

  • Screen land-use history against ARR methodology eligibility criteria.
  • Combine terrain, climate, and existing land-cover layers for each candidate area.
  • Apply species-specific suitability parameters for coffee, cacao, and bamboo to rank eligible areas.

Tools

  • QGIS for spatial overlays and suitability scoring.
  • Geospatial datasets: land use, terrain, and environmental constraints.
  • Multi-criteria scoring combining ARR eligibility and species-specific parameters.

My Role

  • Built the GIS workflow screening ARR eligibility and species-specific suitability.
  • Produced ranked outputs to support restoration-planting prioritization.
  • Sample case study — illustrates methodology, not an actual client deliverable.

Key Takeaways

  • ARR eligibility acts as the first filter — species suitability only matters for areas that already qualify.
  • Coffee, cacao, and bamboo each carry different environmental parameter thresholds, so the same area can rank differently per species.
  • A shared suitability framework lets candidate areas be compared consistently across all three species.
